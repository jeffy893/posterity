But why?

Really — but why?

I've been sitting with a question for a while, and I finally wrote it down. Here's the thesis:

Communicating a perception of someone's developmental stage creates a power dynamic that is not respectful.

Think about it. The neighborhood watch never tells Jim, "fix your lawn and we'll let you in." A family doesn't hand the kids the tax return. And in a hyper-communicative age, whole groups now decide a message is "true enough" for the in-group but needs softening for everyone else. That's not care. That's truth getting warped — usually out of a misplaced, almost parental empathy aimed at other adults.

With age you stop trying to correct every gap you notice. Part of that is wisdom. And here's the tell: when you're getting flack, you're usually on the mark.

So I built a framework I call The Five Respects — mapped in the spirit of C.S. Lewis's The Four Loves. Five genuinely distinct flavors, not degrees of one thing:

→ Man to Man — rooted in risk and the hunt
→ Man to Woman — a different respect entirely, and learning that is a rite of passage
→ Man to Animal — the one I understand least, so I leave it honest and unfinished
→ Man to God — fear and awe; and why there will always be "acts of God"
→ Man to Authority — why it's natural to respect God, and not natural to treat authority as His equal

Underneath it runs a chain that ends in purpose: passion, solvency, and balance alleviate necessity; self-governance plus self-imposed responsibility turns freedom into the choosing of your own responsibilities. Freedom, after all, is just choosing with whom you're in debt.

I'm not going to write the book right now. The market is saturated, and I'd rather share the map than protect it. Maybe the book comes later in life. For now, here's the reference document — diagrams, the openness matrix, a little predicate calculus, and all.

Read it with the same tone I wrote it in: curiosity. Keep asking, but why?
